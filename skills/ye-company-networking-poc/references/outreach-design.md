# Outcome-first outreach design

Draft only after [stakeholder-planning.md](stakeholder-planning.md) has fixed the opportunity, recipient category, evidence grade, desired outcome, and application state. The purpose of a short note is not to summarize the candidate. It is to make one credible next action easy for this recipient.

## Choose the outcome before the words

Pick exactly one primary outcome:

- `direct_hiring_conversation` — confirmed or likely hiring manager;
- `recruiter_screen_or_routing` — assigned or relevant recruiter;
- `referral_assessment` — qualified alum, warm contact, or person who offered referral review;
- `warm_introduction` — person with a verified path to the correct manager/recruiter;
- `team_context` — exact peer or adjacent stakeholder;
- `correct_owner_routing` — senior leader or exact-role sharer who may know the owner.

Do not mix “please refer me,” “tell me about the team,” and “route me” in one invitation. The recipient should understand why them, why this role, why the candidate may be relevant, and the one low-friction next step.

## Recipient strategies

### Confirmed or likely hiring manager

Lead with the exact role and the candidate's closest verified work match. Ask about the most important hiring need, or for a brief conversation when the evidence supports direct ownership. Never claim they are the hiring manager unless `E1`; with `E2`, say the team appears close to their scope.

### Assigned or relevant recruiter

Make screening easy: exact role, one primary match, and a direct question about fit or ownership of the search. The goal is to help the recruiter recognize and route the application to the hiring manager—not to ask the recruiter for a personal referral.

### Same-/higher-level alum, warm contact, or declared referral reviewer

Use the verified relationship basis without overstating familiarity. When the route is warm or the person has explicitly offered referral review, a bounded referral-assessment ask is appropriate. For a cold alum, first establish relevance and ask whether the background fits their referral criteria; do not treat shared school identity as an owed referral.

### Senior leader or router

For a small company, a CEO/CPO/VP/Head may be directly relevant when evidence ties them to the product area. For a large company, use only a scoped unit leader. Give one strong match, then ask for the correct owner. Do not ask a broad C-suite executive at a huge company to manage the application.

### Exact peer or adjacent stakeholder

Ask for team or problem context, or the correct route. Do not imply management authority. A same-level person becomes a referral candidate only when the relationship/evidence supports it.

### `Hiring`-tagged contact

Reference the observed hiring scope only when it is tied to the function or role. Ask whether they own this search or can route it. The tag alone does not prove exact requisition ownership.

## Candidate signal selection

Choose one primary signal that is most useful to this recipient and role; add at most one supporting signal if it materially increases confidence.

1. Extract role needs from the official JD and current team evidence.
2. Extract candidate facts from the verified resume/profile and preserve attribution.
3. Rank candidate facts by recipient usefulness, direct ownership, specificity, and credibility.
4. Select one primary signal; optionally one supporting signal.
5. Omit impressive but irrelevant scale claims.

Examples of attribution-safe phrasing:

- “I owned the design of role-based workflow administration.”
- “I worked on a platform serving 5M users.”
- “I participated in a team project that improved onboarding.”

Do not rewrite the second statement as user acquisition ownership or the third as an individual result.

## Application-state language

Use one canonical current state and reject stale wording:

- `considering` — evaluating the role; no active application preparation claimed;
- `applying` — currently preparing/prefilling; not submitted;
- `applied` — employer confirmation or explicit user report supports submission, with the evidence basis recorded separately;
- `unknown` — current state cannot be verified.

A newer application revision supersedes older outreach wording. Preserve still-current person/team facts, but invalidate “considering” or referral-before-applying copy after the state becomes `applied`. Never allow delayed research output to downgrade a newer application state.

## Invitation structure

Use natural prose, not a rigid template. A useful short note normally contains:

1. exact opportunity/context;
2. why this recipient;
3. one primary candidate match;
4. one recipient-appropriate CTA.

Being direct is not the same as being demanding. State the purpose in the invitation when doing so helps the recipient decide; a context-free “foot in the door” request is usually weaker. Use a softer first step only when the relationship is cold and the requested action needs trust first. Never hide an intended referral ask behind fake curiosity.
