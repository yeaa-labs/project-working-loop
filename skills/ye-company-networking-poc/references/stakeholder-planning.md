# Stakeholder planning for one opportunity

Use this after the exact employer, role, requisition or canonical job URL, team clues, and application state are fixed. The planning unit is:

> **one specific opportunity × one verified recipient × one justified action**

A person is not selected merely because they work at the company. Research and outreach are separate: the research pool may be broad enough to test competing team hypotheses, while only a small, evidence-ranked subset is activated.

## Evidence grades

Grade the evidence for this opportunity, not the person's general prestige.

| Grade | Meaning | Examples | Permitted claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| `E1` | Direct, current evidence tied to the exact requisition or hiring process | named hiring manager; assigned recruiter; exact-role post stating ownership; a current person explicitly offering the relevant referral route | State the evidenced responsibility or offer exactly. |
| `E2` | Strong current team/function evidence; exact hiring ownership remains inferred | leads the exact product area; manages the relevant PM team; recruits product for the exact unit | Call them a likely manager, relevant recruiter, or strong team stakeholder; do not call them the owner. |
| `E3` | Current adjacent evidence that supports routing or informed context | same product group; relevant cross-functional leader; same-or-higher-level alum in the exact organization | Ask for routing, context, or a bounded referral assessment. |
| `E4` | Weak or generic association | company employee, generic alumni tie, broad executive, old directory, search snippet without current underlying evidence | Research lead only. Do not activate unless a user explicitly requests the person and the claim stays narrow. |

A LinkedIn `Hiring` badge is a signal, not a universal `E1`. Tie it to the exact role, function, location, post, or disclosed hiring scope before claiming ownership. Record source, observation date, team/requisition scope, and what remains unknown.

## Candidate categories

Rank verified candidates by useful responsibility, route quality, and evidence grade:

1. Confirmed hiring manager (`E1`).
2. Likely hiring manager or exact-team product leader (`E2`).
3. Assigned recruiter (`E1`) or relevant recruiter for the exact unit (`E2`).
4. Warm introducer or same-/higher-level alum with a credible referral route (`E1`–`E3`).
5. Exact-role peer or adjacent stakeholder who can provide team context (`E2`–`E3`).
6. Exact-role sharer or senior leader who can route the request (`E1`–`E3`).

Do not select a junior alum for a referral ask merely because the school matches. For a referral route, prefer a same-level or more senior alum; a junior person may still be a context source only when there is a specific reason.

## Company-size planning matrix

These are soft planning ranges, not quotas. Stop early when the right route is confirmed, honor an explicit user-requested recipient set, and never manufacture weaker contacts to hit a number.

| Company size | Research pool | Initial activation | Normal ceiling for one requisition | Practical search order |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Under 200 employees | 4–8 | 1–2 | Usually 3 | Confirmed/likely HM; relevant founder/CEO/CPO/VP/Head when the product area is genuinely close to them; assigned/relevant recruiter; same-/higher-level alum or exact peer. |
| 200–1,000 | 6–10 | One active warm route, otherwise 1–2 complementary cold routes | Usually 3–4 | Exact-team leader or likely HM; assigned/relevant recruiter; exact peer/alum; one useful senior router. Avoid defaulting to the CEO unless evidence shows direct product ownership. |
| Over 1,000 | 8–12 across explicit team hypotheses | 1–2 complementary routes | Usually 3–4 | Narrow by business unit, product, location, reporting chain, and requisition clues. Prefer assigned/relevant recruiter plus exact-team leader/peer/alum. Broad C-suite outreach is normally noise. |

For a very large company, do not search the entire company as if all product leaders were interchangeable. Write down the team hypotheses being tested. A result from the wrong unit is not coverage of the target unit.

## Selection output

For every researched person, retain:

- verified identity and current affiliation;
- recipient category and `E1`–`E4` grade;
- evidence source/date and exact opportunity scope;
- chosen action (`activate`, `defer`, `do not contact`, or `await route`);
- intended outcome and CTA;
- conflict/duplication check;
- stop condition.

Keep all explicitly requested recipients in the delivery inventory even if they are deferred or blocked. Never silently replace a requested person with a more convenient person.

## Route and stop rules

- A warm offered introduction is the default single active route while it is usable. Await or use it before activating a cold manager or recruiter route, unless a distinct deadline/process need or explicit user direction justifies one complementary route. Never duplicate the same destination.
- A pending invitation blocks a duplicate invitation. An accepted connection is not a referral or willingness signal.
- Once a correct recruiter or hiring manager gives a useful next step, stop expanding the active set unless a distinct unresolved route remains.
- One recruiter chain should have one active owner unless responsibilities are demonstrably distinct.
- An explicit user request for more recipients expands the delivery set, but stage activation and explain why later recipients are deferred.
- Silence is not a decline. A decline closes that route. A reported referral and an employer-confirmed referral are different states.
